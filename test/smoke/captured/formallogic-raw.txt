### Summary

This is a **three-step direct proof** using a single application of **Modus Tollens**: given the conditional `H → D` (Step 1) and the negation of its consequent `¬D` (Step 2), we derive the negation of its antecedent `¬H` (Step 3) — the health check does not fail. The argument form is the **contrapositive** of the given implication: since `H → D` is equivalent to `¬D → ¬H`, and `¬D` holds, `¬H` must follow.

**Validity** is confirmed — every step follows mechanically from prior steps by a recognized inference rule. **Soundness** is marked `null` because we cannot independently verify the premises from within the proof itself; in practice, soundness depends on whether the health check contract (`H → D`) actually holds in the real system. If a health check can fail for reasons that leave the service fully operational (e.g., a misconfigured probe), then Premise 1 is false and the argument, though valid in form, would be unsound.
