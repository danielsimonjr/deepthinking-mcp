---

**The analysis identifies eight distinct failure modes, all sharing a single root cause: the authentication service was architected with zero resilience primitives.** Every external dependency — the identity provider, Redis session cache, Kafka audit sink, and key store — is called synchronously without a circuit breaker, fallback path, or timeout budget, meaning any one component's degradation collapses authentication for all users. Notably, three of the five highest-severity patterns (p1, p3, p8) are triggered by *planned* operational events (IdP maintenance, key rotation, Redis restarts), not random failures — pointing to a missing operations contract as much as a missing reliability design.

The two highest-priority recommendations are: **(1)** introduce circuit breakers with explicit fallback modes on every external dependency so local-credential users can authenticate without IdP reachability, and **(2)** replace per-pod in-memory rate limiting with fleet-wide distributed counters — the current design offers near-zero brute-force protection at any meaningful fleet size. Estimated completeness is 0.68 because bcrypt tuning, mobile-client retry amplification, and mTLS certificate expiry were not examined; any of the five `wouldChangeAnalysisIf` items could materially shift priority ordering.
