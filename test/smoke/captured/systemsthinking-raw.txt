**The core dynamic here is a classic Limits to Growth archetype, but with two nested vicious reinforcing loops (R1: debt → pressure and R2: headcount → coordination tax) acting as the binding constraints.** The growth engine — hiring more developers — is being strangled by the very structure it was meant to fix: each hire increases coordination overhead quadratically and inherits the full velocity tax of accumulated technical debt.

**The critical insight is that hiring is the symptomatic fix, and it is being applied repeatedly while the fundamental solutions (debt reduction, modular architecture, knowledge documentation) are continuously deferred** — which is the signature of a "Shifting the Burden" dynamic nested inside the larger Limits to Growth. Every sprint where debt reduction is traded away to hit a deadline makes the next hire less effective.

**The highest-leverage interventions are structural, not parametric:** reorganizing into autonomous squads with bounded contexts (breaks R2 topologically) and enforcing a hard-stop on debt accumulation before any new hire is approved (starves R1 of its fuel). Simply adding developers faster, or mandating longer hours, will tighten both loops simultaneously.
